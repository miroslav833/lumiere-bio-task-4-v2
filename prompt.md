Image 1 is the regulatory graph of a published logical model of MAPK signalling in urinary bladder cancer cells. The four pink nodes in the top row are external signals, the three blue nodes in the bottom row are cell fate outputs, and every other node is a protein.

A bladder cancer cell line carries the FGFR3 S249C mutation and has a normal TP53 gene. The cells are kept without any growth factor, they are given cisplatin, and at the same time they are treated with nutlin-3a and SB203580. Find how many of the proteins in Image 1 are active once the network settles.

There are some things which stay fixed. Read the graph as a synchronous threshold model. At time $0$ the only active nodes are the external signals which this treatment switches on and any protein which the genotype or the drugs hold active. At each step every node updates at once from the previous state, and a node becomes active when the number of its active regulators which promote it is greater than the number of its active regulators which inhibit it, and inactive otherwise. Each drawn link counts once with equal weight. A gain of function mutation holds its protein active at every step, a drug which blocks a protein holds that protein inactive at every step, and an external signal keeps its starting value. The network has settled when the state no longer changes from one step to the next. Count every protein which is active in the settled state, including any protein whose state is held by the mutation or a drug, and do not count the external signal nodes or the cell fate output nodes.

Report your final answer as a single integer.
